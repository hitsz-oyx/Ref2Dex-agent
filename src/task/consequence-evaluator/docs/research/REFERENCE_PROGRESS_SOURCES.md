## What to reuse from Google TCC / XIRL

**Causal clips already exist upstream.** XIRL `FrameSampler._get_context_steps` samples trailing context ending at the queried frame, clipping negative indices to frame zero. TCC `datasets.get_steps` uses the same trailing convention. Both explicitly intend to exclude future context. Use eight trailing frames with stride one; do not substitute a centered window. [XIRL frame sampler, lines 68–86](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/xirl/frame_samplers.py#L68), [TCC context sampling, lines 136–147](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/tcc/datasets.py#L136)

**Use the existing squared-distance and temperature convention.** XIRL `pairwise_l2_sq(x1, x2)` computes `torch.cdist(x1, x2).pow(2)`. `get_scaled_similarity` negates the distance, optionally divides by feature dimension, then divides by temperature. `align_sequence_pair` applies row-wise softmax to these logits before forming a soft nearest-neighbour embedding. Flattening a standardized eight-frame geometry clip into one vector lets this small PyTorch helper operate directly without TensorFlow or a visual encoder. [XIRL helper functions, lines 265–307](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/xirl/losses.py#L265)

**The soft temporal index has an upstream analogue.** XIRL `regression_loss` converts cycle-back logits to probabilities and takes the probability-weighted sampled frame indices as `time_pred`. TCC does the corresponding soft cycle-back regression. Our progress uses the *first* actual-to-reference matching distribution, reference index `k/(N-1)`, and a causal history prior. Upstream uses cycle-back indices for a representation-learning loss and normalizes by sequence length rather than our endpoint-inclusive `N-1`; these differences must remain explicit. [XIRL regression loss, lines 460–489](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/xirl/losses.py#L460), [TCC losses](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/tcc/tcc/losses.py)

TCC/XIRL train embeddings by soft nearest-neighbour cycle consistency. Their original alignment helpers compare embedded query steps against all candidate steps; they do **not** themselves impose a bounded forward/backward phase filter. Short context is supplied by the data/model pipeline, not by a hidden monotonic constraint in the distance function. A learned embedding is a possible later stage, not required for the first untrained geometry-label Probe. [XIRL TCC trainer](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/xirl/trainers/tcc.py), [Google's method description](https://research.google/blog/robot-see-robot-do/)

Upstream nearest-neighbour, Kendall tau, and cycle-consistency evaluators are useful diagnostics. Two-way cycle consistency returns from reference to the *whole actual sequence*, so it may inspect actual states later than the queried frame. Do not use that reverse pass or its full-sequence diagnostic as the label distribution for `P_t`. It may be reported separately as offline evidence. [XIRL cycle-consistency evaluator](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/xirl/evaluators/cycle_consistency.py), [Nearest-neighbour visualizer](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/xirl/evaluators/nn_visualizer.py), [Kendall tau evaluator](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/xirl/evaluators/kendalls_tau.py)

**XIRL's deployed reward is different from the requested label.** `compute_goal_embedding.embed` averages final demonstration embeddings; `DistanceToGoalLearnedVisualReward` returns negative distance to this goal embedding, scaled by a demonstration-derived factor. It does not return soft reference index or its 24-step difference. Copying this wrapper unchanged would lose the phase distinction required by ref4_1. [Goal embedding construction](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/compute_goal_embedding.py#L46), [Distance reward wrapper](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/xirl/sac/wrappers.py#L288)

Google's source headers and root [LICENSE](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/a1e7371c5e006f4e8b314bd23d99220d2fe44c51/LICENSE) specify Apache License 2.0. A copied helper must retain attribution and license notice, provide the license text, and identify modifications. Keep the source path and fixed revision beside the adaptation.

## What ROT / OTR contribute, and what cannot be copied

ROT `optimal_transport_plan` constructs uniform marginals across actual and demonstration steps and calls POT Sinkhorn on the complete cost matrix. `POTILAgent.ot_rewarder` embeds both trajectories, computes cosine or Euclidean costs, converts row-wise transport-weighted costs to negative rewards, then picks the demonstration with the highest *whole-trajectory* reward sum. The `method` argument in the inspected rewarder does not switch solvers; the implementation calls `ot.sinkhorn`, transferring the cost to NumPy/CPU. Its global coupling and demonstration choice can depend on later actual steps. [ROT rewarder](https://github.com/siddhanthaldar/ROT/blob/41ef7b98ca3950b9f31dd174f306cbe6916a09c9/ROT/rewarder.py), [ROT agent reward computation, lines 307–364](https://github.com/siddhanthaldar/ROT/blob/41ef7b98ca3950b9f31dd174f306cbe6916a09c9/ROT/agent/potil.py#L307)

The official OTR repository is `ethanluoyc/optimal_transport_reward`. Its `OTILRewarder` separates preprocessing, trajectory packing, Sinkhorn cost computation, reward squashing, and multi-demonstration aggregation. This is a useful interface pattern. `_solve_ot` uses cosine point-cloud costs and OTT-JAX Sinkhorn; `compute_offline_rewards` packs the complete actual episode and can update mean/std from that episode. `aggregate_top_k` selects references using the sum of their episode rewards. Those dependencies violate prefix-only labels if copied unchanged. [Official repository and license description](https://github.com/ethanluoyc/optimal_transport_reward/tree/006e910a1d84c94bf855480328c490444bd4517b), [OTR implementation](https://github.com/ethanluoyc/optimal_transport_reward/blob/006e910a1d84c94bf855480328c490444bd4517b/otr/agents/otil/rewarder.py)

Even rerunning OT on successive prefixes does not justify recomputing an earlier `P_t` with later actual observations. Marginal rebalancing can change earlier alignments. If OT becomes a later diagnostic, each `P_t` must retain its own prefix-specific result and frozen normalization; a full-episode coupling is never its substitute. This is an inference from the inspected coupling/preprocessing code. Also, neither cosine costs nor unconstrained OT resolve repeated-state phase ambiguity by themselves.

## RARM: related idea, different objective

The official project describes learned rollout-clip/reference-clip comparisons and confidence gating. The gate is calibrated by reference self-comparisons. Its displayed accumulated reward accepts only confident matches that exceed the highest previous progress, producing a nondecreasing reward total. That is different from our signed `P_(t+24)-P_t`, which must allow regression. The page showed **Code Coming soon** at this reading; no full training repository was linked there. Do not claim that no code can exist anywhere, or that our geometry matcher reproduces its visual comparator. [Official project page](https://rarm-robotics.github.io/)

## Recommended first implementation and checks

These are proposed adaptations to the user's supervision contract, not properties established by the upstream papers:

1. Freeze successful references and feature scales before the label run, using reference/training data only. Preserve reference IDs, source hashes, coordinate transforms, keypoint ordering, units, and sampling frequency. Exclude a reference episode from the evaluation set. Actual full-episode normalization would leak future information even with a causal matcher.
2. Extract object pose, all 11 measured object-frame hand keypoints, and backward-difference object and relative hand motion. Use the same geometric definitions for references. Do not replace absent reference hand points with zeros or differently ordered human joints.
3. Reuse XIRL trailing-context sampling and scaled squared-distance helper on standardized eight-frame clips. Retain all reference stages, including the final normal placing segment. Calculate likelihoods without actual-frame indices, actual episode length, reward flags, or old S/P/M as inputs.
4. Add a separately documented sequential prior permitting advance, stay, and retreat. Enforce a meaningful per-step phase-jump bound on the posterior output; a banded latent transition alone can still allow posterior expectation jumps when previously tiny distant modes gain likelihood. Do not impose monotonic progress or an automatic forward drift with elapsed actual time.
5. Define `P_t` as the probability-weighted `k/(N-1)` and freeze each causal result when produced. Form `Y_t` only when all 24 executed transitions are present and both endpoints have valid matching confidence. Low confidence should be marked, rather than silently converted into zero or forced advance.
6. Check prefix invariance by truncating or replacing everything after `t+24`; both endpoint progress estimates and their difference must remain identical. Also check stationary histories, genuine retreat, repeated start/end states, exact-reference self-alignment, nominal success, and unsuccessful rollout clips. A causal eight-frame clip does not logically guarantee unique phase: identical clips still require history and may remain ambiguous.
7. Validate these labels on a small train-only selection before fitting an evaluator. Candidate rankings require a margin and abstention. Cross-episode windows at different starting histories are association evidence; they do not establish same-state counterfactual action ordering without a comparable-history pairing contract.

The cheapest informative first Probe is therefore **fixed-reference geometry alignment on existing raw rollouts**, with causal-invariance and phase-ambiguity diagnostics. Training a representation or implementing whole-trajectory OT first would add cost before establishing whether the requested scalar labels are credible.
